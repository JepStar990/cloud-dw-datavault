Why this matters

None of these six APIs were designed to work together. The World Bank doesn't know about Open-Meteo. Wikimedia doesn't know about OpenAQ. USGS doesn't know about GitHub. They have different schemas, different time grains, different coordinate systems, and different update cadences.

The Data Vault 2.0 architecture makes them joinable — not through fragile foreign keys or hard-coded mappings, but through hash keys derived from business keys (country codes, coordinates, article titles). Every new source enriches the city profile without touching existing models.

Adding a 7th source requires zero schema changes — new hub, new satellite, join through existing PIT tables. That's the architecture pitch.

Real-world applications

Environmental justice — overlay air quality with population density to identify at-risk communities.

Disaster response — when a quake hits, did Wikipedia traffic spike? Did GitHub contributors in the region go silent?

Climate migration — 65 years of population data joined with weather trends. Are warming regions seeing population shifts?

City dashboard — mayors and planners get a single pane of glass across domains that typically live in separate departments.
